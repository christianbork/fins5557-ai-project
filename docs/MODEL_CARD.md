## Intended use and scope
- **Use:** a **second-stage screen** for consumer loan applicants who already pass the lender's basic policy
  checks. It gives a calibrated probability of default (PD), an approve / refer / decline decision and the
  main reasons, and drafts a letter for an analyst to check.
- **Not for:** first-line screening of all applicants, other products (mortgages, business loans), other
  countries or periods without revalidation, or fully automatic declines without an analyst's review.
- **Why the scope:** the model learnt only from loans Home Credit had already approved (selection bias), so it
  has never seen the applicants their policy turned away. Inputs outside the training range are referred to an
  analyst rather than approved.

## Data and terms
- Home Credit Default Risk, `application_train.csv` (Kaggle, 2018): 307,511 applications,
  8.1% with payment difficulties (the target) on the test split.
- Used under the competition's rules (https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/home-credit-default-risk/data): the data is not redistributed, so it is not in the
  repository, and the app shows only invented or synthetic applicants and aggregate statistics.
- Split (stratified, seed 100): 184,506 rows training,
  61,502 rows validation (model choice, calibration, cut-off), 61,503 rows
  test (reported once).
- Amounts in USD (display assumption: Home Credit's data doesn't state its currency).

## Inputs
- **23 raw inputs**, all known at the time of
  application: Annual income, Loan amount, Regular repayment, Price of goods financed, Children, Household size, Days in current job, Days since registration details changed, Days since ID document was renewed, Car age (years), Region risk rating (lender's own), External credit score 1, External credit score 2, External credit score 3, Credit bureau enquiries (last year), Loan type, Owns a car, Owns property, Income type, Education, Housing, Occupation, Type of employer.
- **9 engineered features** from them (`src/features.py`): affordability
  ratios, loan term, job tenure, the mean and number of missing external scores, income per person.
- **Deliberately excluded:** defaults in the applicant's social circle (judging someone by their friends is
  hard to justify), building statistics and document flags (mostly empty or not meaningful to an analyst).

## Protected attributes
Sex, age, family status are **never model inputs**. They are kept only to audit the decisions for fairness
(below). Other inputs can still carry them indirectly: the external credit scores are correlated with age (a
longer credit history scores better).

## Training and tuning
- Candidates: logistic regression, random forest and XGBoost. Randomised search (30 settings per tree model), full grid for logistic regression,
  3-fold stratified CV on the training split, chosen on AUC
  (`roc_auc`); class weighting was tuned too.
- Model choice: the best validation AUC wins, but a tree model must beat logistic regression by 0.01 to be
  chosen over the more transparent scorecard. XGBoost has the highest validation AUC (0.757), 0.017 above logistic regression (0.740), which clears the 0.01 margin needed to justify a more complex model.
- Chosen settings: `colsample_bytree` 0.7802, `learning_rate` 0.0359, `max_depth` 5, `min_child_weight` 20, `n_estimators` 486, `scale_pos_weight` 3.3745, `subsample` 0.7756.

## Cut-off and assumptions
- Expected loss = PD × LGD × EAD, with loss given default (LGD) 60.0%, a lifetime net margin of
  15.0%, and exposure at default (EAD) = the loan amount. Break-even PD = margin / (margin + LGD) =
  20.0%.
- Decision rule: **Cost ratio 5:1 (Hofmann, 1994)**, cut-off **16.7%**. PDs within
  2.0 pts of it are referred to an analyst: approve below 14.7%, decline above
  18.7%. Test split: 84.9% approve, 6.8% refer,
  8.3% decline; realised profit $3.59bn.
- LGD and margin are stated assumptions, not estimated from the data. Three decision rules were compared on the
  test split (reports/threshold_comparison.csv): Profit, 4:1 (Thomas, 2000): cut-off 21.0%, 25.5% of defaulters caught, 94.8% of good customers approved, profit $3.62bn; Cost ratio 5:1 (Hofmann, 1994) (used): cut-off 16.7%, 41.3% of defaulters caught, 88.7% of good customers approved, profit $3.59bn; Balanced accuracy (Brodersen et al., 2010): cut-off 8.7%, 67.1% of defaulters caught, 71.2% of good customers approved, profit $3.18bn.
- **Lending policy limits (stage 1, before the model):** an application is declined by policy if the loan
  would take more than 48 instalments to repay, the repayment is larger than income, or the
  loan is more than 30 times income. They sit just beyond the range of Home Credit's
  approved loans, because the model can't extrapolate there; the test results above are the model's alone.

## Fairness (test split, automatic decisions)
| Attribute | Groups judged | Lowest disparate-impact ratio | Groups flagged |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sex | 2 | 0.92 (Male) | 1 |
| Age band | 5 | 0.74 (Under 30) | 4 |
| Family status | 5 | 0.88 (Single / not married) | 4 |
| Education | 4 | 0.86 (Lower secondary) | 3 |

1 group falls below the four-fifths rule (disparate-impact ratio 0.80). 12 groups have an equalised-odds gap above 5.0% and 11 groups a parity gap above 5.0%. The largest gap between mean PD and the actual default rate in any group is 1.2%, so the PDs are calibrated within groups: the gaps follow real differences in default rates, and when default rates differ a model can't be both calibrated within groups and equal on error rates. Groups with fewer than 100 test applicants are listed but not judged.

## Limitations
- **Selection bias:** trained only on approved loans; unreliable for applicants unlike past approved ones
  (handled by the out-of-range guard and the second-stage scope).
- **Ranking power is moderate** (test AUC 0.757): application data alone can't separate
  defaulters well; most defaulters are still approved at the profit cut-off.
- **Age proxy:** creditworthy younger applicants are declined more often than older ones, mainly through the
  external credit scores. Whether to accept, reduce or offset this is a policy decision.
- **Drift:** a static model; the Monitoring page checks each input with PSI, and calibration should be
  rechecked on recent outcomes before relying on it after a shift.
- **Target:** "payment difficulties" as defined by Home Credit, not a regulatory default definition.
- **Currency and period:** the dataset's currency and dates aren't stated; amounts are shown in USD as a
  display assumption.
- **Letters:** drafted by a template or a language model with guardrails; always a draft for an analyst.
